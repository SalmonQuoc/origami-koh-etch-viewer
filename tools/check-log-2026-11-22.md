What changed (engine/src/sim.rs and src/engine/etch-engine.js, kept bit-identical, tests/engine-parity.test.mjs):

1. Narrow-band leak (speed). The Lax-Friedrichs dissipation lowered φ slightly below the band limit B at the band edge; those
   nodes joined the band, and over a run the band grew into most of the grid (883 000 of 1 024 000 nodes in the dx 2 pit). The
   re-initialisation now resets band nodes it does not reach to ±B, and it counts the etchant above an opening (ghost value of
   the top row) as a crossing, so the front at the surface is kept. The band in that run is now 126 000 nodes.
2. Second-order differences (accuracy). One-sided differences use the ENO second-order correction with the minmod limiter
   (Osher & Shu 1991); time stepping is Heun's method (two forward-Euler stages averaged, TVD Runge-Kutta 2). Next to the top
   boundary (k < 2) the z differences stay first order. Cost: two stages per step.
3. Re-initialisation seeds (accuracy). Fast marching started from distances estimated from linear crossings on both sides of the
   front, which moved the front a little every 6 steps. On {111} walls the rate rises steeply with the off-angle (R(221) =
   0.714 µm/min only 15.8° away), so these small moves thinned Si walls by several µm whatever the grid. Etched-side seeds now
   keep their value, so the front stays where the time step put it.
4. Rust kernel: unchecked array reads in the time step (about 12 % faster natively).

Tried and dropped: a linear-extrapolation boundary under the mask (undercut overshoots at the top, 46–52 µm against 31.9);
phi/|∇φ| seeds (Russo & Smereka 2000 style; depth 49.7 µm, walls thinned again); keeping both seed sides (undercut 26.5 µm at
every grid, depth lag); band width 3 cells (25 % faster, slightly larger depth lag; kept at 4).
