    How this scene etches a shape

    Each wall is a crystal plane that moves out from the opening at its own rate (n·x ≤ h + R·t). A non-convex shape (L, U, cross, frame, ring, star) is split into overlapping convex pieces; each piece etches as a convex opening and the cavity shown is their union. Concave corners therefore stop at {111} as they should, but convex mask corners (the inside corner of an L or U, the tips of a star) are not undercut here, although they are in real KOH etching (Kovacs et al. 1998, Fig. 1(c); Pal et al. 2007, Sec. 2). Openings that grow into each other are overlaid, not merged. The etch simulator handles both.

    Columns: w, h = size; t = arm width (L, U, cross, frame) or inner size (ring, star); n = sides or points; array = copies nx × ny at pitch px, py (µm).

    Model, sources and assumptions
    • Rates (sourced): KOH 30, 40, 50 wt% at 70 °C for 12 orientations, Pal & Sato Table 4.1, p. 87. KOH 42 wt% at 75 °C: {100} 42, {110} 66, {111} 0.5 µm/h, Lec 4-1 p. 7; its other 9 orientations are the Pal & Sato 40 wt% values scaled by R(100) (assumption).
    • Rates between the measured orientations (assumption): linear interpolation over a triangulation of the 12 orientations in the standard triangle (gnomonic coordinates), with cubic symmetry. It keeps the cusp at {111}: rates grow linearly with the off-angle, as measured for vicinal (111) (Pal & Sato p. 104).
    • Corner rules (sourced, not imposed): concave corners show the slowest plane and convex corners the fastest (Lec 4-1 p. 8). The simulator does not apply these rules; they come out of the rate map.
    • Front motion (method): every surface element moves along its normal at R(hkl); this is solved as a 3D level set (first-order Lax-Friedrichs scheme, fast-marching re-initialisation) on a cubic grid. Accuracy is about one cell; sharp edges are rounded over about two cells. Check: square window on (100), 60 min in 30 wt%: depth 47.9 µm against 0.797 × 60 = 47.82 µm; {111} wall 54.5° against 54.74°.
    • Mask edge (assumption): no etchant reaches silicon through the mask, so silicon under the mask is only removed from the side. At the top row under the mask the level set has no downward slope; where the wall there is near-vertical (slope below 0.35), the vertical gradient is set to zero. Check: a 100 µm square window at 45° on (100), 40 min in 30 wt% KOH, gives 26.5 µm sideways undercut at a 2 µm grid (15.5 µm at 4 µm) against 0.797 × 40 = 31.9 µm (Pal & Sato Table 4.1, p. 87); a <110>-aligned pit keeps 0 µm. Use a fine grid when {100} undercut matters.
    • Known numerical limits: silicon walls and beams narrower than about 8 grid cells thin slowly (numerical dissipation, not physics), most when they are oblique to the grid; windows narrower than about 5 cells etch too slowly. Use “fine” quality or wider features when this matters. Undercut values are good to about one to two cells.
    • Mask (assumption): inert and infinitely thin; etchant reaches the silicon only through openings and under released mask. The SiO₂ loss shown for 42 wt% uses 0.34 µm/h (thermal oxide, Lec 4-1 p. 7).
    • Wafer: 525 µm thick by default (100 mm wafer, Pal & Sato Table 2.1, p. 30); only the depth that the etch can reach is simulated, and the back side is protected (assumption). Frames: (100) x = [1̄10] along the flat (Rev 1); (110) two conventions, see above; (111) flat along [11̄0] (assumption).
    • Pre-etch (assumption): a vertical prism of the opening shape, as by deep RIE (Lec 4-1 p. 16), before KOH.
    • Compensation data (sourced): ⟨100⟩ beam width W = 2d (Pal & Sato p. 249); ⟨110⟩ beam d = (0.5 W cos α + L sin α)/Ur (Pal & Sato p. 244); KOH turns a 90° convex corner into 150 ± 2° (Pal & Sato p. 235). Example dimensions not covered by these are design choices.

    Find the true crystal orientation

    The flat of a real wafer is only good to 2–5° (Lec 10 p. 18; 1° to 5°, Pal & Sato p. 294), so mask edges drawn along the flat miss ⟨110⟩ by a flat error δ. Here the crystal is turned by δ. Put test structures on the mask, etch them, read them under the microscope, and turn the device mask by the estimate before the device etch (Lec 10 pp. 18, 25–26; Pal & Sato Ch. 7).

    Lec 10 and Pal & Sato Ch. 7 give these methods for (100) and (110) wafers. Pick (100) or (110) above.

    Method, sources and assumptions
    • Flat error (sourced): ⟨110⟩ flat 2–5° (Lec 10 pp. 18, 25); 1° to 5° (Pal & Sato p. 294). Pre-etched patterns reach 0.01° (Pal & Sato p. 296). Misalignment widens a channel of length l by U = l sin δ cos δ (Pal & Sato eq. 7.1, p. 289: 18 µm for 10 mm at 0.1°).
    • Slot fan (method after Lec 10 p. 18, “long slot gives around 1°”): long slots at stepped angles. In the long-etch limit a slot of length L and width w misaligned by α etches to the ⟨110⟩ rectangle of width w cos α + L sin α (3 cm at 1° gives 524 µm, Lec 10 p. 18). The narrowest slot gives δ to ± half a step; the widths of its two neighbours give a V-shaped fit. Fan size, step and spacing are design choices.
    • Circle arc (sourced): circles on an arc of radius R at pitch p, so neighbours turn by p/R. On (100) each circle etches to an inverted pyramid with ⟨110⟩ edges; the pair whose line of centres is parallel to ⟨110⟩ has aligned edges (Ensell, in Pal & Sato pp. 297–299: 41 circles Ø 75 µm, 78.5 µm apart, 0.1°, R = 45 mm; Lec 10 p. 18: 80 µm pitch, tan⁻¹(80/45000) = 0.1°). On (110) the circles etch to hexagons and the bow-tie corners of neighbours line up (Tseng and Chang, Lec 10 pp. 25–26: φ/360° = (√6 r + x)/(2πR), R = 48.9 mm, 0.06°). Here the edge offset between neighbours k and k+1 is read as the shift of their edges along the true edge normal, p sin(τ − δ).
    • Inconsistency in the source: Lec 10 p. 25 gives 0.06° with a 35 µm pitch and R = 48.9 mm, but 35/48 900 rad = 0.041°. The lecture layout here uses r = 20 µm and x = 2 µm, so p = √6 r + x = 51 µm and φ = 0.060° at R = 48.9 mm (design choice that matches the stated 0.06°).
    • Etching (method): each structure is etched with the analytic plane model of the Etched pit (walls n·x ≤ h + R t; long-etch limit: {111} only), with the crystal turned by δ. Structures do not interact (cavities that grow into each other are overlaid, not merged).
    • Reading (assumption): every length is rounded to the readable length. Real limits not modelled: mask-writer angle steps (Chang and Huang, in Pal & Sato p. 304), edge roughness, rate scatter, θ error of the second alignment.
    • Random wafers: δ uniform in ±5° (upper end of Lec 10 p. 18); readings from the long-etch formulas above, which the analytic etch reproduces (checked).
    Scenes 1 and 2 (model and pit): each wall is a crystal plane that moves outward at its own rate from the mask opening (n·x ≤ h + R·t over the {100}, {110} and {111} planes); the long-etch limit keeps only {111} planes with R = 0. This is the construction used in the origami tutorial; non-convex openings are split there into overlapping convex pieces whose cavities are joined, and convex mask corners are not undercut; use the etch simulator for that (its model and sources are listed in its panel). Rates: Pal & Sato, p. 87 (KOH 30 wt% and 40 wt%, 70 °C); Lec 4-1, p. 7 (KOH 42 wt%, 75 °C). Primary flat ⟨110⟩ for (100) and (110): Lec 4-1, p. 6. For (111) the flat is assumed along [110]. Wafer thickness 525 µm (assumption). x runs along the flat, z is the wafer normal.
