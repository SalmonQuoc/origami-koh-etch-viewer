    Model, sources and assumptions
    • Rates (sourced): KOH 30, 40, 50 wt% at 70 °C for 12 orientations, Pal & Sato Table 4.1, p. 87. KOH 42 wt% at 75 °C: {100} 42, {110} 66, {111} 0.5 µm/h, Lec 4-1 p. 7; its other 9 orientations are the Pal & Sato 40 wt% values scaled by R(100) (assumption).
    • Rates between the measured orientations (assumption): linear interpolation over a triangulation of the 12 orientations in the standard triangle (gnomonic coordinates), with cubic symmetry. It keeps the cusp at {111}: rates grow linearly with the off-angle, as measured for vicinal (111) (Pal & Sato p. 104).
    • Corner rules (sourced, not imposed): concave corners show the slowest plane and convex corners the fastest (Lec 4-1 p. 8). The simulator does not apply these rules; they come out of the rate map.
    • Front motion (method): every surface element moves along its normal at R(hkl); this is solved as a 3D level set (first-order Lax-Friedrichs scheme, fast-marching re-initialisation) on a cubic grid. Accuracy is about one cell; sharp edges are rounded over about two cells. Check: square window on (100), 60 min in 30 wt%: depth 47.9 µm against 0.797 × 60 = 47.82 µm; {111} wall 54.5° against 54.74°.
    • Mask edge (assumption): no etchant reaches silicon through the mask, so silicon under the mask is only removed from the side. At the top row under the mask the level set has no downward slope; where the wall there is near-vertical (slope below 0.35), the vertical gradient is set to zero. Check: a 100 µm square window at 45° on (100), 40 min in 30 wt% KOH, gives 26.5 µm sideways undercut at a 2 µm grid (15.5 µm at 4 µm) against 0.797 × 40 = 31.9 µm (Pal & Sato Table 4.1, p. 87); a <110>-aligned pit keeps 0 µm. Use a fine grid when {100} undercut matters.
    • Known numerical limits: silicon walls and beams narrower than about 8 grid cells thin slowly (numerical dissipation, not physics), most when they are oblique to the grid; windows narrower than about 5 cells etch too slowly. Use “fine” quality or wider features when this matters. Undercut values are good to about one to two cells.
    • Mask (assumption): inert and infinitely thin; etchant reaches the silicon only through openings and under released mask. The SiO₂ loss shown for 42 wt% uses 0.34 µm/h (thermal oxide, Lec 4-1 p. 7).
    • Wafer: 525 µm thick by default (100 mm wafer, Pal & Sato Table 2.1, p. 30); only the depth that the etch can reach is simulated, and the back side is protected (assumption). Frames: (100) x = [1̄10] along the flat (Rev 1); (110) two conventions, see above; (111) flat along [11̄0] (assumption).
    • Pre-etch (assumption): a vertical prism of the opening shape, as by deep RIE (Lec 4-1 p. 16), before KOH.
    • Compensation data (sourced): ⟨100⟩ beam width W = 2d (Pal & Sato p. 249); ⟨110⟩ beam d = (0.5 W cos α + L sin α)/Ur (Pal & Sato p. 244); KOH turns a 90° convex corner into 150 ± 2° (Pal & Sato p. 235). Example dimensions not covered by these are design choices.
    Scenes 1 and 2 (model and pit): each wall is a crystal plane that moves outward at its own rate from the mask opening (n·x ≤ h + R·t over the {100}, {110} and {111} planes); the long-etch limit keeps only {111} planes with R = 0. This is the construction used in the origami tutorial; non-convex openings are not modelled there, use the etch simulator (its model and sources are listed in its panel). Rates: Pal & Sato, p. 87 (KOH 30 wt% and 40 wt%, 70 °C); Lec 4-1, p. 7 (KOH 42 wt%, 75 °C). Primary flat ⟨110⟩ for (100) and (110): Lec 4-1, p. 6. For (111) the flat is assumed along [110]. Wafer thickness 525 µm (assumption). x runs along the flat, z is the wafer normal.
